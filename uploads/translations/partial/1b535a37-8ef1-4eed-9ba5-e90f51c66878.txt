Hyperion

The Consul of Hegemony sat on the balcony of his ebony-black spaceship and played a prelude in D-flat minor by Rachmaninoff on his well-preserved "Steinway", while below, echoing the music, was the lowing of enormous green pseudojaguars stomping through the boggy muck. From the north came a storm. Against the leaden clouds that blocked out the sky with a nine-kilometer wall, the outline of a forest of giant vegetative trees became visible. A flash of lightning crisscrossed the horizon. Nearby, in the misty blue air, vague figures of reptiles would occasionally appear, attempting to penetrate the protective field but vanishing with a roar at once. The Consul focused on the complex section of the prelude, oblivious to the approaching storm and deepening darkness.

The multiline receiver buzzed.

The Consul's fingers froze over the keyboard as he turned his attention to the call. A thunderous roar rolled across the sweltering air. From the forest came a mournful howl from a pack of scavengers. Below, in the darkness, some mindless creature let out a piercing shriek. Suddenly, silence fell—the only sound was the hum of the protective field. Then the receiver buzzed again.

"Damn it!" the disgruntled Consul muttered as he answered the call.

In those few seconds while the computer processed and decrypted the tachyon pulse packet, the Consul had just enough time to pour himself a whisky and, when the indicator flashed green, settle into the cushions in the projection niche.

"Activate," he commanded.

"A decision has been made regarding your return to Hyperion," replied a raspy female voice. The image was still forming: the niche's air flickered, traced with lines of encoded signal that the Consul knew came from Tau Ceti—the administrative center of Hegemony. However, to confirm this, the Consul didn't need coordinates. No one's voice could be mistaken for that of Maya Gladston, though hers was aged but still beautiful. And now she told him:

"A decision has been made regarding your return to Hyperion to participate in the pilgrimage to Shraik."

"More?" the Consul thought and stood up, preparing to leave the niche.

"You and seven others have been selected by Shraik's church and approved by the Altling," said Maya Gladston. "Hegemony is interested in your consent."

The Consul froze at the doorway; behind him, the flickering line continued. Without turning around, he raised his glass to his lips and drained the whisky.

"The situation is very complex," continued Maya Gladston. Her voice sounded weary. "Three standard weeks ago, the consulate and the Local Governance Committee informed us via multiline that the Time Tombs seem to be opening. The anti-entropic fields around them are expanding rapidly: Shraik has been seen near the Bridle, and he's moving further south."

The Consul turned and sat back down on the cushions. The holographic projector finally activated, and Maya Gladston's wrinkled face appeared in the air, her eyes reddened from fatigue.

"To evacuate the citizens of Hegemony on Hyperion before the Time Tombs open, we immediately sent a special squadron from Parva with our WCS. They'll be there within three Hyperion years, maybe a little later." Maya Gladston paused, and the Consul thought he'd never seen the Senate secretary so grim. "We hope the evacuation team will make it in time," she said, "but another factor complicates things. A migration group of Brigands, consisting of at least four thousand... units, has been detected approaching Hyperion." The Consul understood why Maya Gladston hesitated. A Brigand migration group could consist of ships of any class—from single-seater scouts to floating cities and comet fortresses capable of housing tens of thousands of space savages.

"The Unified Command believes the Brigands are on the offensive," said Maya Gladston. The computer arranged the hologram so that it seemed her sad, caramel-colored eyes were directly staring at the Consul. "What remains to be seen is whether their target is only Hyperion and its Time Tombs, or if they intend to move further and attack the Network." Just in case, a full-strength combat fleet with an attached engineering nul-T battalion had taken off from System Kamn to meet the evacuation team, but it could be recalled at any moment.

The Consul nodded and absentmindedly raised his empty glass to his lips. Frowning, he spun it in his hands, then threw it on the thick, soft carpet of the niche, where it shattered. Unfamiliar with military strategy, he nevertheless understood the immense complexity of the tactical dilemma Maya Gladston and the Unified Command were facing. To repel the Brigand invasion into the Hyperion system, a nul-T transport portal had to be built there within a short time (at enormous effort!). Otherwise, the secrets of the Time Tombs would fall into the hands of enemies of Hegemony. If this portal was constructed on time and Hegemony threw all its forces at defending the remote colonial outpost, the Brigands could break through the weakened perimeter of the Great Network at another point and even—in the worst-case scenario—seize an operational nul-channel and penetrate the Network. The Consul saw in his mind's eye hordes of these savages spilling out of nul-portals onto the streets of unguarded cities on hundreds of planets.

He passed through Maya Gladston's hologram, raised his glass, and headed for another helping of whisky.

"You are included in the group selected for the pilgrimage to Shraik," said the old dignitary, often compared in the press to Lincoln, Churchill, or Alvarez-Temp (depending on which legend from the era before Hijra was most popular at the time). "The Templars are providing us with their starship tree 'Iggdrasil.' The commander of the evacuation forces has ordered it to be allowed passage." It would take three weeks for the ships to reach Parva; 'Iggdrasil' would meet your ship and depart immediately on a quantum leap. The other six pilgrims chosen by Shraik's church would already be aboard 'Iggdrasil.' Intelligence data suggests that at least one of the seven pilgrims is a Brigand agent. We... currently... have no way to identify him."

Unwittingly, the Consul smiled. In addition to all the troubles that had fallen on Maya Gladston, she now had to consider the possibility that the Consul was this very spy and that she was passing strategically important information to a Brigand agent. But had she revealed anything truly crucial? As soon as the ships' engines fired up, the fleet's movements became public knowledge; if the Consul really were a spy, he would have seen the revelations as an attempt to scare him off. The smile faded from his face, and he drained the whisky in one gulp.

"The pilgrims include Sol Weintraub and Fedman Kassad," said Gladston.

The Consul's eyebrows furrowed deeper, and he looked at the cloud of numbers flickering like dust motes around the image of the old woman. The connection had fifteen seconds left.

"We need your help," finished Maya Gladston. "We must unravel the mystery of the Time Tombs and Shraik. This pilgrimage may be our last chance. If the Brigands capture Hyperion, their agent must be destroyed, and the Time Tombs sealed forever—at any cost. It depends on the fate of Hegemony."

The transmission ended. On the screen remained only a line of coordinates for the meeting place with 'Iggdrasil.'

"Reply?" asked the computer.

Despite the immense energy expenditure, the spaceship could cram into its unceasing roar of superluminal conversations that linked the inhabited corners of the galaxy, and send its compressed message in a short pulse.

Lenar Hoyt was then a young priest, just ordained. He had been born and raised on Catholic Pasem and now left for the first time his homeland: he was accompanying Father Paul Duere, a respected member of the Jesuit order, into quiet exile on the colonial planet Hyperion.

In another time, Father Paul Duere, no doubt, would have become bishop, or even pope. He was a tall, thin man with an ascetic appearance. And Father Duere was convinced that the Roman Catholic Apostolic Church remained humanity's last and truest hope for immortality.[5]

These structures were formed by weakening of the crust, which for geological epochs periodically froze and thawed. During warmer periods, underground rivers like Kens slowly eroded it from within. Soon Tuk joined me, out of breath. I undressed and, after quickly wiping off the stench of tar and smoke from my robes and travel clothes, splashed around in the cold water.
