We need to choose three people out of eight without regard for gender or age group distinction (since it's just specified that there are two men and six women). The formula for combinations is C(n, k) = n! / [k!(n - k)!], where "!" denotes factorial, the product of all positive integers up to a given number.

So we have: 8 people (total), choose 3 members for the committee (k=3). This gives us our total possible combinations without any gender or age group restrictions as follows: C(8, 3) = 8! / [3!(8 - 0)] = 56.

Now we need to subtract from this number those committees that do not meet the criteria of having at least one man and two women. This can happen in three ways: no men (which is impossible since there are only two), exactly one woman, or all women which also isn't possible as it would mean choosing 3 out of 6 without any restriction on gender but we need to have a minimum of one male member for the committee and thus this scenario can be ignored.

C(6, 2) = 15 combinations for each case where one man and two women make up a committee (since there are only two men available in total): C(8,1)*C(6,2)=2*15=30 committees that do not meet the criteria.

Therefore, subtracting these from our original 56 possible combinations gives us: 56 - 30 = 26 valid committees where at least one man and two women are present on it which satisfies all given conditions in problem statement.
