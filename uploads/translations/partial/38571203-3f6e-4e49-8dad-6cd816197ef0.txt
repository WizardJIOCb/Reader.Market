**EINS**

Was für eine Absteige! Gut, ich hatte gewusst, dass es nicht das Grand Elysée sein würde. Aber diese Unterkunft ist wirklich das Letzte. Eine Zumutung. Muffig und dunkel. Und dreckig. Ich gebe mir Mühe, mich nicht genauer umzuschauen, aber der Schmutz meiner Vorgänger ist unübersehbar. Es ist offensichtlich, dass hier schon ziemlich lange nicht mehr saubergemacht wurde. Mir ist zum Heulen zumute - wie konnte ich nur in eine solche Lage geraten? Heute Morgen noch im Salon von Schloss Eschersbach, und jetzt das. Nun fange ich wirklich an zu heulen.

"Schnauze, du nervst!", kommt es keine zwei Sekunden später von links.

Richtig: Das Schlimmste hatte ich noch nicht erwähnt: meine Zimmernachbarn. Fünf an der Zahl, die meisten von ihnen unglaublich verwahrlost. Und das nicht nur optisch. Ungebildeter Pöbel, der in einem Adligen wie mir natürlich gleich ein willkommenes Opfer ausgemacht hat. Mein Stammbaum reicht bis 1723, diese Ignoranten neben mir kennen vermutlich nicht einmal den Unterschied zwischen Markgraf und Markklößchen.

Vor meinem inneren Auge taucht mein Großvater auf. pflegte er zu sagen. Ach Opili, wenn du mich jetzt sehen könntest - ich bin definitiv ganz unten angelangt. Bei diesem Gedanken heule ich noch lauter. Irgendjemand muss mich hier einfach rausholen!"Wo ein von Eschersbach ist, ist oben. Vergiss das nie!"

"Komm, Süßer, beruhige dich.", sagt eine Hand und krault mich hinter den Ohren. "Gleich gibt es ein leckeres Fresschen, und dann sieht die Welt ganz anders aus. Der erste Tag ist für alle schlimm."

Hm, eine nette Stimme. Interessiert gucke ich zur Seite, um festzustellen, zu wem sie gehört: Neben dem Zwinger steht eine junge Frau in einer Latzhose und lächelt mich aufmunternd an. Ihre Hand riecht nach gewöhnlichem Dosenfutter, trotzdem hat die Berührung etwas Tröstliches. Ich schlecke ihre Finger ab, sie beginnt zu kichern.

"Ja, ja, das schmeckt dir, nicht wahr?", flüstert sie mir zu.

O je, wenn die wüsste - an meinen verwöhnten Dackelgaumen habe ich bisher eigentlich nur frisches Herz und Pansen gelassen. Fertigfutter war die absolute Ausnahme, das gab es wirklich nur, wenn Emilia, unsere Köchin, krank war oder Urlaub hatte. Bei dem Gedanken an Emilia krampft sich mein kleines Herz zusammen, und ich muss ein bisschen winseln. Als ich mich heute Morgen von ihr verabschiedet habe, hat sie geweint. Weiß der Teufel, wie Menschen das immer hinbekommen. Aber zum ersten Mal in meinem Leben hätte ich viel dafür gegeben, auch ein paar Tränen vergießen zu können.

"Du Armer, noch so schlimm?", fragt die Pflegerin mitfühlend. "Mach dir keine Sorgen, du bist so süß, wir finden bestimmt bald ein neues Frauchen oder Herrchen für dich. Versprochen!" Dann streicht sie mir noch einmal über den Kopf und zieht ihre Hand wieder durch das Gitter.

Ich drehe mich um und trotte in die andere Ecke des Zwingers. Dort wirft ein Sonnenstrahl noch ein einladend helles Fleckchen auf den Boden, und ich beschließe, es mir ein bisschen gemütlich zu machen.

Offenbar bin ich nicht der Einzige, der auf diese Idee gekommen ist: Bevor ich mich hinlegen kann, tritt mir ein riesiges schwarzes Irgendwas direkt auf die Pfoten.

"Kleiner, ich glaube, du verziehst dich besser auf die andere Seite. Hier ist mein Platz.", sagt es heiser und knurrt.

Was für eine lächerliche Töle! Meint die ernsthaft, sie könne mich in die Flucht schlagen? Mich, dessen Ahnen noch mit dem letzten Kaiser zur Jagd gegangen sind? Ich schüttle den Kopf.

"Ich glaube nicht", entgegne ich so würdevoll, wie es mir unter diesen widrigen Umständen möglich ist, "dass in diesem Etablissement mit Reservierungen gearbeitet wird. Ich war vor Ihnen da, also werde ich mich auch auf diesen Platz legen. Sie gestatten?" Mit diesen Worten schiebe ich Mr. Irgendwas zur Seite und lege mich schnell hin. Er starrt mich völlig fassungslos an. So viel zivilen Widerstand hat er wohl noch nie erlebt. Zufrieden räkle ich mich. Opili hatte doch Recht - ein von Eschersbach ist eben auch oben, wenn er unten ist.

Während ich noch darüber sinniere, wann hier wohl mit der zweifelsohne eher bescheidenen Mahlzeit zu rechnen ist, verfinstert sich mein sonniges Fleckchen. Nanu, eine Wölke? Ich blicke nach oben, um festzustellen, was in aller Welt hier auf einmal diesen unerfreulichen Schatten wirft - und schaue direkt in das Gesicht eines ziemlich ungemütlich aussehenden Boxers. Er schiebt seine Nase ganz dicht an meine und verströmt dabei einen Geruch, dass es mir regelrecht den Atem verschlägt.

"Pass auf, du aufgeblasener Zwerg: Wenn du nicht neu wärst, wärst du jetzt ein toter Hund. Hier gelten unsere Regeln, besser du hältst dich daran. Also wenn mein Freund Bozo sagt, dass du dich verpissen sollst, dann...", er kommt noch ein bisschen näher und schnappt blitzschnell nach mir.

Aua! Ein stechender Schmerz fährt durch mein rechtes Ohr. Hilfe! Der ist ja gemeingefährlich! Ich belle aufgeregt - offensichtlich bin ich unter militante und gewaltbereite Straßenköter geraten. Aber sosehr ich auch belle - niemand kommt. Nicht einmal die junge Frau in der Latzhose. Boxer und Bozo grinsen selbstzufrieden.

"Spar dir die Mühe. Die hört dich momentan nicht - ist zu den Katzen rübergegangen. Wir könnten dich jetzt richtig plattmachen, und niemand würde dir helfen. Ein toter Hund mehr in der Statistik dieser Bude. Wen interessiert das schon?"

Ich merke, wie sich meine Nackenhaare sträuben und es mir eiskalt den Rücken herunterläuft. Bozo, die schwarze Töle, baut sich wieder vor mir auf.

"Also, was ist jetzt? Wenn ich sage verpiss dich?"

"Dann verpisse ich mich?", ergänze ich seinen Satz.

"Richtig. Hundert Punkte. Braves Hundchen."

Bozo verpasst meiner empfindlichen Nase noch einen kräftigen Stüber mit seiner ungepflegten Pfote. Erschreckt springe ich zur Seite und laufe auf zittrigen Beinen in die andere Ecke des Zwingers. Dort sitzen noch zwei andere Hunde, die das Geschehen gelangweilt beobachtet haben. Mord und Totschlag scheinen hier an der Tagesordnung zu sein, jedenfalls interessiert sich niemand dafür, dass ich gerade Opfer eines Verbrechens geworden bin. Ein älterer Münsterländer rückt ein Stück zur Seite, als ich mich neben ihn setze. Immerhin nicht gleich der Nächste, der mich bedroht. Eine Weile hocken wir schweigend nebeneinander. Dann rückt er mir verschmitzt zu und murmelt: "So gut es eben geht, haha. Ich heiße übrigens Fritz."

Ich sage erst einmal nichts. Unter den gegebenen Umständen habe ich wirklich keine Lust, mich zu unterhalten. Stattdessen lege ich den Kopf auf meine Pfoten und sehe Bozo und dem Boxer zu, wie sie sich auf Sonnenfleckchen fläzen. Wahrscheinlich machen sie sich gerade über mich lustig. Eigentlich bin ich sehr gerne ein Dackel, aber in diesem Moment wäre ich viel lieber ein Kampfhund. Staffordshire, Pitbull oder irgendetwas anderes in Richtung meinem Lizenz zum Töten.

"Don't be sad," Fritz nudges me, "the nurse just said you're such a real person type, someone will take you out of here soon. And then you'll show those two idiots over there the middle finger because no one wants them."

I look at Fritz thoughtfully. Hopefully he's right.

The next morning I feel like a rag doll. I barely slept—and even when my eyes closed for five minutes, I had terrible nightmares. Of boxers and pit bulls chasing me through the kennel, and vast amounts of canned food that tasted absolutely disgusting. Tired, I hobble to Fritz who wags his tail at the cage door.

"Morning," I ask him, "why are you so awake and in such a good mood?"

"Well, today's visitor day. And if someone actually comes looking for a dog, I want to make a good impression. I'm not getting any younger, so it's important to seem dynamic and cheerful."

Is he right? Honestly, I have no desire to put on the trained dachshund act. But the thought of settling in here for an extended stay is terrifying. So I stand next to Fritz, wagging my tail unmotively back and forth. And would people really fall for such a cheap trick? Unbelievable.

"By the way," Fritz asks, "what's your name?"

"Carl-Leopold," I reply brusquely.

"Carl-Leopold? That's a strange name for a dog."

"Not to me. It depends on where you come from," I boast proudly.

"From Eschersbach?" Fritz grunts, still wagging his tail.

I sigh. What a snob! He has no idea about beautiful names. "I'm from Eschersbach," I add haughtily.

"Eschersbach? That doesn't ring a bell for me." Fritz just mumbles and continues wagging his tail.

Suddenly, my heart quickens. Not because of the noise—after all, this place is so noisy that a dachshund's delicate ears should fall off—but because of an indescribable smell wafting through the air. Even Fritz seems to have caught a whiff, stopping his silly wagging and pressing his snout against the bars instead.

"Do you smell that?" I ask him. He nods. "Amazing, isn't it?"

"Yes, incredible!" I agree.

"It's the most beautiful scent I've ever smelled on a person," I conclude.

There's no doubt: this belongs to a human. Every dog knows immediately. But who is this human? Not some grubby meathead like sausage or chocolate cookie. No, more like... I scratch my head. ...It's got to be... oh yes—a beautiful summer day. A happy summer day. Lots of flowers, a hint of strawberries, and a wisp of mint. Fantastic.

"Probably we'll be disappointed when we see the person," Fritz says matter-of-factly.

"Really?" I want to know. "I haven't noticed any connection there myself. I can't judge."

"Oh yes, absolutely," he insists.

We gaze expectantly at the door. And there she comes, followed by her overalls. Fritz was completely wrong. Because for a human, she's gorgeous, like an angel. She chats with the other woman and laughs while doing so. Her eyes laugh too—which looks especially beautiful and is rare among humans. Most of them just crinkle their mouths when they laugh. Which is a shame. So if I could laugh, I'd make my eyes do it too. It definitely looks better that way.

"Hmm," Fritz says, "so you want a smaller dog, maybe a younger one?" The angel nods.

Fritz's ears droop immediately. He knows what that means: no lady for him again. Because Münsterlanders aren't small—and Fritz is far from young. He hangs his head. "Good luck," he whispers to me before trotting past. Of course, I feel bad about it—but maybe this is really my chance? I try Fritz's tactic again, wagging my tail excitedly and trying to bark as cheerfully as possible. In fact, the two women are now walking straight towards me.

"This is our junior," she says, "just got him. About six months old."

She extends her hand through the bars, and I lick it right away. Well, if that doesn't make a good impression, I don't know what does. The angel bends down to my level.

"Well, what a cutie!" she exclaims, bouncing up and down.

"Yes, such a handsome fellow," I reply, wagging my tail energetically.

"A dachshund mix," she says.

Ouch. Mix. Ugh. That stings. I immediately stop acting like an excited dog. Not that it wouldn't be true—on the contrary. Miss Overalls is right. And in doing so, she hits on my shame: I am a mutt. The result of Mom's affair with a very dashing terrier. Precisely why I'm here. Because although I am Carl-Leopold von Eschersbach, I'm not a purebred dachshund—not suitable for hunting and certainly not breeding. That's what the old lord of Eschersbach said before he put me in a box and drove me here. Emilia cried, but she already had my sister, and two dogs were too much for her.

Apparently I've started whining, because now the angel extends her hand through the bars and pets me.

"Oh, poor thing," she coos, "are you sad?"

How embarrassing. An Eschersbach doesn't cry! And then not in front of such a beautiful woman! Heaven forbid where this is going to end? But apparently that was exactly what I needed, because now she stands up, points at me, and says: "I want him. Absolutely. Can I take him right away?"

Overalls nods. "Come on in," she says, "then we'll take care of the formalities. He's been vaccinated, comes from a very meticulous breeder. A little accident, that's all."

She chuckles at her own joke. And I would love to give her a good lick across the face—but I don't. Otherwise, I might have to stay here.

Twenty minutes later, I'm snugly tucked away in the box on the back seat of Carolyn's car. Carolyn—that's what I've decided to call my angel. I found out when saying goodbye. Carolyn. A beautiful name. So noble. Probably—no, definitely—Carolyn comes from a noble family. Something like that is obvious to me as a dog. And Carolyn is in good spirits. She whistles a tune and occasionally checks the rearview mirror to see how I'm doing.

"Well, my sweetie," she says, "now you'll get to know your new home. I'm very curious to see what you think."

And I am too! Will it be as nice as Schloss Eschersbach? With a big park? And lots of rabbit hutches? The car slows down, then comes to a stop. Carolyn opens the door and pulls out the box. Now I have strawberries and mint right in front of my nose, and I would love nothing more than to lick Carolyn from head to toe. But I still have to wait before I can get out of this swinging box.

My surroundings darken and sway more violently; Caroline carries me up a flight of stairs. I try to sniff out my new home through the bars of the box with my nose. In any case, it seems like various people and animals live here. At least one cat immediately comes to mind.

Now Caroline sets down the box, and I hear her unlocking a door. She pushes the box further with her foot, then fiddles with the lid, opens it, and carefully lifts me out.

"Voilà! This will be your home from now on. Take a good look around, little guy."

At first, I see nothing—it's so bright in here. I blink cautiously and try to adjust to the light. Dimly, I recognize that we're likely standing in a human living room. In front of the window sits a large couch; it looks like a tiny dachshund could very comfortably take a nap there. Does Caroline allow that? Anyway, it was strictly forbidden at Schloss Eschersbach. Which naturally led my sister and me to leap onto the sofa in the salon for no other reason than because it was hilariously funny when the old master of the house, despite his cane, flew by us like greased lightning and wildly swished his stick around to shoo us away.

I bound towards the sofa and sniff its fabric. Hm, strawberries and mint as well? But something else too. No animal. More like a human. I dive deeply into the scent. Hm, not only do I have a new mom, but also a dad? This isn't a woman's scent, that much is clear. While I'm still pondering, Caroline lifts me up and sets me down—yes! yes! yes!—on the sofa, right next to her. Enthusiastically, I lick her hands—this woman clearly knows what dachshunds love. She laughs and pulls her hands away. Then she looks at me thoughtfully.

"Well then, little guy, I've taken care of everything for you: a crate, leash, food bowl, food. The only thing left is..." I shake my head; in my opinion, that sounded pretty complete. "You need a nice name."

I squeak surprised—I already have a name! Or did Caroline simply abandon me at the animal shelter without telling me anything about myself? Such heartlessness!

Evidently sensing my indignation, Caroline lifts me onto her lap, and we lock eyes.

"Hm, well then, what kind of little guy like you should I call you? What do you look like?"

I try to puff out my chest and look as dignified as possible. Maybe she'll think of Carl-Leopold on her own. To emphasize this point, I bark majestically twice more. Come on, Caroline, think hard!

Yes! Exactly! She's got it! With a majestic toss of my head, I declare, "I'm Herkules!"

What? HERKULES? An ancient Greek instead of old aristocratic?

TWO

Herkules! Okay, Caroline might not have much taste when it comes to dachshund names, and I'll need some time to get used to this strange new name. But she definitely has a knack for finding the right neighborhood. In fact, the house I now live in seems almost as large as Schloss Eschersbach. My feeling that Caroline comes from good stock seems to be correct. The neighbors don't exactly reside in modest cottages either. Right behind our house begins a park. I'm not sure, however, if it belongs solely to Caroline because it's enormous. When we take a short walk there, I can't even make out where the park ends—fantastic!

And it's not just big; adventure awaits within it as well. Even after just a few steps, I sense rabbits and squirrels. Instantly, I want to run off, but then a sharp tug on my neck reminds me that Caroline has something for me that is completely unfamiliar: a kind of rope she's attached to my collar. Aaaauu! What in the world is this? I turn around, take the thing in my teeth, and tug a bit on it. Caroline kneels down next to me.

"Well then, Herkules? Don't you like your new leash? Or have you never gone for a walk like this?" She's not sure if a little dog like me can do that. Walking on a leash, I mean. But unfortunately, there's a leash law here, and she can't just let me run around freely.

At the mention of "leash law," I bite down a bit harder on the rope. I don't exactly know what it means, but it definitely sounds like something aimed against dogs.

"Oh, come now!" Caroline says gently, then strokes my head. I let go of the leash and look at her.

"I guess I'll have to buy a book on dog training or maybe book some hours with a trainer? After all, you're my very first dog ever. But yesterday, I suddenly had the feeling it would be nice to have such a friendly, loyal companion around."

Okay, that training business is obviously unnecessary nonsense, and I hope Caroline figures it out on her own. But the part about being friendly and loyal definitely applies to me. To all of Eschersbach's dachshunds, in fact. I'd even go so far as to say we're famous for it. Opili says so, I think gloomily. What happens if the human suddenly leaves the dog? That was something Opili would never have thought of, I add mentally. A momentary bad mood tries to take hold of me, but then Caroline rustles something in her pocket. Hmh, not just anything—I recognize this scent! It's sausage. She actually holds a piece of it under my nose.

A dachshund from Eschersbach never leaves his human. Never! Remember that, Carl-Leopold!

"Here you go, my dear. Let's start our first training session with something enjoyable. I hope you like this."

I grab the sausage tip and jump up and down excitedly. Caroline should know that was the right idea.

"That makes you happy, doesn't it? Maybe we'll skip leash training for now and instead visit Daniel. It's time you met him. Around this time of day, he's probably hard at work and might appreciate a little variety."

I would have liked to stay in the park a bit longer—even with a leash. Perhaps we would have encountered another dog I could have quizzed about the neighborhood. But if Daniel is important... well then!

Caroline takes the same route back that we came, and I make an effort to walk bravely behind her on the leash. Maybe I'll get another sausage piece if I give her a successful demonstration now. In fact, she does turn around briefly.

"Good boy, Herkules! You're learning quickly!" She praises me, unfortunately without reaching into her pocket again. That's okay; I'm not really hungry anyway.

Now we're back in front of our house. Does Daniel live here too? Caroline bends down and picks me up onto her arm.

"Time for the workshop!"

THREE

I'd rather plug my ears or hide under the couch. Because what I'm experiencing right now fills me with real fear. A man I've never seen before has just entered our apartment—or more precisely, Carolin's apartment—and started yelling immediately. I'm speechless. Who is this horrible guy? And where is Daniel? Won't he protect his woman? Maybe I should look for him quickly so he can help Carolin. But that would mean passing by the yelling man, and frankly, I don't dare. Is it really a bad habit to hate people who can be so loud? My ears are truly remarkable. For my taste, neither humans themselves nor their insistent music should be so noisy. But that's just me.

In his aggressive manner, this man reminds me a lot of Bozo and Boxer. He's huge, several inches taller than Carolin. And his hair is as black as Bozo's fur. He makes the same haughty movements and has the same uncultivated tone. Apparently, he's a proletarian through and through, just like Bozo-Boxer.

The man waves his arms wildly around and points—at me! Oh no!

"Did you buy a dog? I've only been back from a business trip for three days, and you go and get one?"
